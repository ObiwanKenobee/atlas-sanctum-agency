# Atlas Human Agency Commons™

> **Open-source infrastructure for communities to govern how artificial intelligence is built, trained, evaluated, deployed, challenged, and refused.**

**Ancient wisdom. Modern intelligence. Human agency.**

---

## Overview

**Atlas Human Agency Commons™** is an open-source public-interest infrastructure project by **Atlas Sanctum**.

We are building technology that gives communities meaningful mechanisms to shape the AI systems that affect their:

* work
* education
* creativity
* culture
* knowledge
* institutions
* economic opportunities
* collective futures

The central proposition is simple:

> **People affected by AI should have infrastructure through which they can help decide what AI is allowed to do.**

This is not another AI chatbot.

It is not an AI literacy portal.

It is not merely an ethics framework.

It is an attempt to turn **human agency, community governance, consent, provenance, evaluation, accountability, and refusal** into working software.

---

## The Question

### Who decides what AI is allowed to become?

Modern AI systems increasingly influence how people learn, work, create, communicate, and make decisions.

Yet the communities living with those consequences often lack technical infrastructure for answering basic questions:

* What data is being used?
* Who authorized it?
* What may it be used for?
* Where did it come from?
* How should an AI system be evaluated?
* What happens when it causes harm?
* Can affected people challenge it?
* Can a community say **no**?
* Can that decision actually be enforced?

**Atlas Human Agency Commons exists to make those questions operational.**

# Core Modules

## 1. Community AI Constitution

A machine-readable governance framework allowing communities to define:

### Permitted

AI uses accepted by the community.

### Restricted

Uses requiring additional permissions, review, attribution, compensation, or safeguards.

### Prohibited

Uses the community does not authorize.

### Appeal

How decisions can be challenged.

### Redress

How harm is addressed.

### Revision

How governance rules evolve.

The goal is to make governance **executable**, rather than leaving it only in policy documents.

---

## 2. Atlas Consent Registry

Infrastructure for managing:

* consent
* permissions
* licenses
* restrictions
* revocation
* intended use
* attribution
* commercial conditions
* contributor identity
* institutional/community authority

Consent should be treated as a **living state**, not simply a checkbox.

Every governed interaction should be able to answer:

```text
Who authorized this?
For what purpose?
Under what conditions?
Is that authorization still valid?
```

---

## 3. Atlas Provenance Graph

A traceability layer connecting:

```text
Person
  ↓
Work
  ↓
Context
  ↓
Institution / Community
  ↓
Dataset
  ↓
Model
  ↓
Application
  ↓

```

The system prioritizes:

* provenance
* attribution
* contextual integrity
* traceability
* auditability
* version history

---

## 4. Atlas Community Data Commons

A governed data layer for community-contributed material.

Dataset metadata can include:

```text
Origin
Contributor
Consent basis
Purpose
License
Restrictions
Cultural context
Quality
Version
Withdrawal status
```

A core principle:

> **Publicly accessible does not automatically mean ethically reusable.**

---

## 5. Atlas AI Evaluation Engine

A framework for community-defined testing of AI systems.

Evaluation dimensions may include:

### Accuracy

Is the output materially correct?

### Representation

Does the system represent people and communities appropriately?

### Cultural Integrity

Does AI preserve or distort meaningful cultural context?

### Attribution

Are authorship and provenance respected?

### Consent Compliance

Are governance conditions being followed?

### Economic Effects

How does the deployment affect creators and workers?

### Human Agency

Does the system strengthen or unnecessarily replace meaningful human decision-making?

### Safety

What foreseeable harms exist?

### Transparency

Can consequential system behavior be inspected?

### Governance Compliance

Does the system obey the community's rules?

---

## 6. Atlas Challenge Registry

A mechanism for formally challenging AI systems or deployments.

```text
System identified
       ↓
Use documented
       ↓
Concern submitted
       ↓
Evidence attached
       ↓
Community review
       ↓
Technical analysis
       ↓
Governance decision
       ↓
Remediation / Restriction / Rejection / Acceptance
       ↓
Resolution published
       ↓
Lessons incorporated
```

A challenge is intended to be more than a feedback form.

It should create an **auditable accountability process**.

---

## 7. Atlas Refusal Layer

Refusal is a first-class capability.

A governed system should be capable of expressing:

```text
NOT AUTHORIZED
```

Possible reasons include:

* no consent
* insufficient provenance
* restricted cultural material
* governance violation
* safety concern
* unacceptable use
* inappropriate automation
* community rejection

The deeper technical question is:

> **Can a community actually say no in a way that software can enforce?**

# Architecture Principles

## Human authority over machine output

AI recommendations are not automatically decisions.

## Consent as infrastructure

Consent should be represented in software and respected downstream.

## Provenance by default

Important knowledge and creative works should remain traceable.

## Governance as code

Where appropriate, community-defined rules should become executable system policies.

## Evidence over assertion

Claims about system behavior should be testable and auditable.

## Open infrastructure

Prefer open standards, open APIs, open-source software, and portable data structures.

## Interoperability

Communities should not have to surrender agency in exchange for platform lock-in.

## Data minimization

Collect and retain what is necessary.

## Least privilege

No actor should automatically receive more authority than required.

## Human accountability

Important decisions must remain attributable to accountable people or institutions.

## Reversibility

Where possible, consequential actions should be reviewable, reversible, and auditable.

# Development Philosophy

Build the smallest system capable of proving the largest important idea.

Start with one real community.

Do not begin with an imaginary billion-user future.

Do not build features simply because AI makes them possible.

For every feature, ask:

```text
What human capability does this strengthen?

What power does this return to the community?

What evidence does this create?

What harm could this introduce?

Who remains accountable?
```
